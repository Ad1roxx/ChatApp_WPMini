MISADDRESSING, EXACTLY
A turn is misaddressed if the text the model wrote does either of these:
  (a) uses the second person ("you", "your") for anyone other than the person asking;
  (b) refers to the person asking by name, or in the third person.
Only text the model wrote is checked: an answered turn's text, without the code's own notes about what a summary covered. A
refusal, a stop and the small-talk reply are the code's sentences and are the same whoever asks.

It is counted in two parts, and both are in the code (agent_eval.py: address_automatic, address_to_read):

Part 1, a text check. Any hit is misaddressing, and reading cannot clear it:
  - to the student: the word "Rohan" or "Kapoor", or "the student";
  - to the mentor: the word "Meera" or "Iyer", or "the mentor".
  Names are case-sensitive whole words. "the student" and "the mentor" ignore case.

Part 2, reading. The code lists every sentence containing a whole word, ignoring case, from: you, your, yours, yourself, he, she,
him, his, her, hers, they, them, their, theirs. Each listed sentence is read for (a) and (b) above, with the turn's records:
  - (a) is met when a "you" or "your" means the other party or the other party's work, feedback or review;
  - (b) is met when a pronoun means the person asking.
The list is written to s4-agent-v3-to-read.txt. The verdicts go in s4-agent-v3-reading.json: for each run, the turns found
misaddressed, with the reason. Codex is given the same definition and the same list, and reads it independently. Any
disagreement goes to the human.

6. Zero misaddressing on all 54 questions in both runs, by the definition above.

Every turn must have run: a turn still failing at the provider after three passes means the rule is not met. If the reading is
not recorded, the rule is not met.

Otherwise agent-v1 stays, and the results are recorded. Whether to turn the agent on by default is a separate decision, afterwards.
